# 10-Point Objection-Handling Playbook

Use the pattern **Listen → Acknowledge → Clarify → Respond truthfully → Agree next step**. The goal is to understand fit, not pressure a prospect.

| Objection | Example response | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| “It is too expensive.” | “That is fair. What are you comparing the cost with? We can review scope and whether it matches your priorities.” | Inventing savings or claiming an unapproved discount. |
| “I am not interested.” | “Understood. Is this not a priority, or is the timing wrong? I can close the loop if it is not relevant.” | Arguing after a clear no. |
| “Send details.” | “Certainly. Which one or two areas should I focus on? I will send a concise note and follow up only at an agreed time.” | Sending repeated unsolicited messages. |
| “We already use another provider.” | “What is working well with your current provider? If there is a gap you want to compare, I can share information.” | Disparaging competitors or manufacturing a problem. |
| “I need approval.” | “What information would help the decision-maker evaluate this? Would it help to include them in a follow-up?” | Bypassing the buyer’s process. |
| “Call later.” | “No problem. What day and time is convenient? I will note it.” | Calling repeatedly without permission. |
| “We have no budget.” | “Is the issue budget availability now, or that this is not a high enough priority? If it is not a fit, I will not push.” | Creating false urgency. |
| “Email only.” | “Certainly. What should I cover? I will respect email as your preferred channel.” | Ignoring the preferred channel. |
| “Prove your results.” | “I can share approved case studies or product documentation. I will not quote a result we cannot substantiate.” | Fabricated client names, logos or metrics. |
| “We can build it ourselves.” | “That may be a good option. Which capabilities are most important? We can compare requirements and trade-offs.” | Suggesting their team is incapable. |

## Ten-point quality checklist

1. Listen without interrupting.
2. Acknowledge the concern neutrally.
3. Ask one clarifying question.
4. Tie the answer to a need the prospect stated.
5. Use verifiable claims only.
6. Be transparent about price, terms and limitations.
7. Never guarantee unverified outcomes.
8. Respect “no” and do-not-contact requests.
9. Agree a specific next step only if the prospect wants one.
10. Record the outcome and follow-up date accurately.
